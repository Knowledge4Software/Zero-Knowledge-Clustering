\section{Introduction}
Prototype banks summarize feature vectors in clustering and learned
representation systems. A candidate bank fitted to new training data can
improve training distortion while worsening a held-out catalog. Before a
release, the model owner may therefore ask whether proposed centers reduce
nearest-center squared distance on the same committed evaluation features.
A proof of an approximate computation answers a different question when
its accuracy interval cannot establish the ordering. We study certificates
that resolve the sign of the exact quantized cost change, or explicitly
request further computation.

Our release-batch workflow holds the feature extractor and deployed bank
fixed while a provider submits several candidates. For example, an owner
can compare prototype updates from two newly arrived training minibatches
and three update rates before choosing one release. The catalog commitment
and preprocessing are shared across these requests. Our added experiments
instantiate this workflow with smartphone sensor features and a frozen
transformer encoder for text; the owner commits the encoder outputs.
After a release installs a new reference bank, its groups must be
preprocessed again. This differs from accepting and rebasing after every
candidate, whose costs are analyzed separately.

The auditor verifies approved commitments, centers and proof outputs rather
than receiving an original-row input file. This is an aggregate-disclosure
interface: the data owner must authorize the disclosed statistics and their
inferences. In particular, authorized probes can recover group moments and
can expose singleton records. We make no claim of record non-reconstruction
or of hiding the underlying feature data against those permitted outputs.
The certificates also do not attest encoder inference or classification
accuracy. A trusted-hardware design such as Slalom~\cite{tramer2019slalom}
offers a different trust choice; here verification relies on the stated
cryptographic and setup assumptions.

Future proposals arrive after preprocessing and may change every center.
They need not belong to a preapproved bank. The deployed reference remains
fixed during an audit episode; installing another reference requires new
group preprocessing. Exact affine scores for a bank known in advance are a
useful alternative under a different contract, reported in the appendix.

Our mechanism separates the exact cost change under deployed-model groups
from the correction due to switching to other new centers. Authenticated
group counts and sums evaluate the first part. A compact representation,
residual energy and a correction for rounded basis vectors bound the second.
If the aggregate interval contains zero, authenticated exact checks on at
most 16 rows tighten it before a newly generated exact Full fallback.
Figure~\ref{fig:audit-pipeline} summarizes this path. The original rows and
derived statistics are linked, and the circuit checks exact old memberships.

The contribution is a verifier-enforced decision mechanism: exact old-group
cost change, an integer-safe switching envelope for a rounded basis,
complete authenticated catalog coverage, and bounded exact-row refinement
before paid fallback. PCA and a centroid-informed basis instantiate the same
relations. Projection identities, norm inequalities, commitments and
Groth16 are established components
\cite{feldman2013tiny,feldman2020tiny,cohen2015dimensionality,groth2016,poseidon22023}.

The evaluation asks three questions: whether switching information resolves
decisions left open by authenticated moments, whether the resulting audit
saves measured proof cost after fallback, and how the benefit changes with
the learning workload. A retained ablation resolves 19/19 updates where
moments-only resolves 2/19. Real-proof comparisons also certify naturally
worsening updates with switching. Prediction agreement, individual-label
certificates and update decisions are evaluated as separate outputs.

\subsection{Relationship to prior work}
The distinction from the closest ingredients is the certified decision
interface. Feldman et al. supply projection-plus-residual clustering
summaries~\cite{feldman2013tiny,feldman2020tiny}, and Cohen et al. analyze
dimension reduction for $k$-means~\cite{cohen2015dimensionality}; those
identities and approximation principles are not claimed as new. V3DB audits
approximate vector search on a committed snapshot~\cite{v3db2026}, whereas
ZK-Value targets locality-based Shapley data valuation~\cite{wang2026zkvalue}.
Here the statement is a full-catalog interval for the difference between two
specified quantized nearest-center costs. Authenticated exact old groups,
rounded-basis bounds and verified corrections make a strict sign decision
checkable even when moments alone are inconclusive. The mechanism, rather
than a new projection identity or a better learned predictor, is the claimed
increment.
Clustering certificates based on semidefinite relaxations concern global or
approximate optimality~\cite{iguchi2016certifiably,mixon2017certificates}.
Our certificate concerns the cost ordering of a specified update.

Private and verifiable clustering, zero-knowledge closest-point queries and
statistics-based data valuation already have substantial precedents
\cite{duan2008private,patel2013clustering,zhang2021verifiable,ghosh2016queries,wang2026zkvalue}.
V3DB authenticates approximate vector-search execution on a committed snapshot
\cite{v3db2026}. We instead output an interval for exact quantized cost change,
then resolve its sign or retain an inconclusive result. Committed-snapshot
auditing itself is not new.

General verifiable ML offers important alternative implementations. zkCNN
specializes convolution proofs; ZEN and ZKML optimize inference circuits;
Mystique supports efficient representation conversions; zkLLM targets large
language-model inference; and EZKL uses a Halo2-based compilation workflow
\cite{zkcnn2021,feng2021zen,chen2024zkml,weng2021mystique,sun2024zkllm,ezklsoftware}.
Artemis addresses reusable commitments for zkML~\cite{artemis2024}.
These works make a comparison with one Groth16 circuit insufficient to claim
superiority over modern exact proving generally. GKR-style protocols, folding
and proof aggregation offer additional design directions
\cite{thaler2013time,xie2019libra,kothapalli2022nova,gailly2022snarkpack}.
Our current timings compare implemented complete paths; unimplemented
backends are not assigned hypothetical speedups.

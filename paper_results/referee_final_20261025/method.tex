\section{Reusable group cost-change certificates}\label{sec:group-design}
\subsection{Task and preprocessing contract}
Let $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$, with $x_i\in\mathbb Z^D$, be the fixed committed
integer catalog and let
$C^0=(c^0_0,\ldots,c^0_{k-1})$ be the deployed centers. For approved future
public centers $C^1$ with the same count and index correspondence, the task is
to bound
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=\Phi(C^1)-\Phi(C^0),\qquad
 \Phi(C)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\min_j\|x_i-c_j\|^2.
\end{equation}
The audit covers the complete catalog. Its group totals do not authorize a
different subset, and changing the deployed comparison model requires new
preprocessing. Future center values need not belong to a preapproved bank or
the represented span. Input quantization and clipping are fixed before the
queries. The statement concerns quantized cost, not unrounded input data,
class-label accuracy or correctness of model training.

Preprocessing proves a private exact old membership
$g_i\in\arg\min_j\|x_i-c^0_j\|^2$. Ties are allowed. Define
$n_g=|\{i:g_i=g\}|$ and $S_g=\sum_{i:g_i=g}x_i$. The same preprocessing binds
these private moments, each membership, and the compact statistics to the
committed original rows. Exact old membership is proved inside the circuit;
choosing plausible groups only in native code would not justify the method.

\subsection{Exact assigned change and switching bounds}
The moments give the exact fixed-membership change
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group-assigned-change}
 A=\sum_g\left[2\langle S_g,c^0_g-c^1_g\rangle+
 n_g(\|c^1_g\|^2-\|c^0_g\|^2)\right].
\end{equation}
For each row define the nonpositive switching advantage
$b_i=\min_j\|x_i-c^1_j\|^2-\|x_i-c^1_{g_i}\|^2$. Exact old membership yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group-change-identity}
 \Delta=A+\sum_i b_i.
\end{equation}
Thus the high-dimensional assigned contribution is exact, and only changes
in the winner need an uncertainty envelope. This is a stronger preprocessing
contract than authenticating head coordinates alone.

For rounded integer basis $B$, scale $T$, and fixed anchor $a$, store
$H(x)=TB(x-a)$ and $E(x)=\|R(x)\|^2$, where
$R(x)=(T^2I-B^\top B)(x-a)$. Let $G=BB^\top-T^2I$ and
$\mathcal D(x,c)=\|H(x)-H(c)\|^2+E(x)+E(c)$. Exact expansion gives
\begin{align}
 T^2\mathcal D(x,c)-T^6\|x-c\|^2
 ={}&2T^2\langle R(x),R(c)\rangle\nonumber\\
 &+(H(x)-H(c))^\top G(H(x)-H(c)).
\end{align}
Subtract distances to two new centers. The quadratic Gram terms in $H(x)$
cancel, leaving a verifier-derived affine expression and a residual cross
term. Writing $h_j=H(c^1_j)$, $e_j=E(c^1_j)$ and
$\delta h=h_j-h_g$, the complete affine expression is
\begin{align}\label{eq:explicit-affine}
 q_{ijg}(H_i)={}&2H_i^\top(G-T^2I)\delta h\nonumber\\
 &+T^2(\|h_j\|^2-\|h_g\|^2+e_j-e_g)\nonumber\\
 &-(h_j^\top Gh_j-h_g^\top Gh_g).
\end{align}
The verifier computes these coefficients from the approved centers and basis,
including the rounded Gram matrix. Put
$F_{ijg}=T^6(\|x_i-c^1_j\|^2-\|x_i-c^1_g\|^2)$.
With conservative integer ceiling radii,
\begin{equation}
 \left|F_{ijg}-q_{ijg}(H_i)\right|
 \le 2T^2\lceil\sqrt{E_i}\rceil
       \lceil\|R(c^1_j)-R(c^1_g)\|\rceil.
\end{equation}
Taking the minimum over $j$, including the exactly zero $j=g$ term, produces
integer endpoints $\ell_i\le T^6b_i\le u_i\le 0$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group-envelope}
 T^6A+\sum_i\ell_i\ \le\ T^6\Delta\ \le\ T^6A+\sum_i u_i.
\end{equation}
All arithmetic, signed comparisons and ranges are checked; the bounds do not
assume that rounded $B$ is orthonormal. A negative upper endpoint certifies a
strict improvement and a positive lower endpoint a strict worsening.
The interval $[0,0]$ certifies equality; any other interval containing zero
is inconclusive. The selected profile evaluates all switching batches to
produce its reported two-sided interval. Its finite-workload interval widths
are reported separately from its sign-decision coverage.

\subsection{Bounded authenticated exact-row refinement}\label{sec:refinement}
An inconclusive compact interval need not immediately require a complete Full
query. For that candidate only, order rows by decreasing canonical switching
interval width, with index breaking ties, and authenticate exact corrections
on at most 16 rows. Stop at the first conclusive interval. The order uses the
compact bounds, not exact switching values or the true update sign. The cap
is a development choice, not a universal accuracy threshold. The same policy
and budget apply to PCA. Any remaining inconclusive candidate receives newly
generated exact Full proofs.

Let $\bar\ell_i,\bar u_i$ denote the canonical compact endpoints, computed
with $\bar\rho_i=\lceil\sqrt{E_i}\rceil$. A refinement proof opens the original
row and its derived $(H_i,E_i,g_i)$ leaf at the same index and approved roots.
It checks the minimal integer radius, reconstructs the compact endpoints,
and computes $b_i$ exactly from the original row and approved new centers.
It publishes the row index and signed endpoint corrections
\begin{align}
 d_i^-&=T^6b_i-\bar\ell_i\geq0,\\
 d_i^+&=T^6b_i-\bar u_i\leq0.
\end{align}
For the unique refined index set $J$, replace the aggregate endpoints by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refined-interval}
 [L',U']=\left[L+\sum_{i\in J}d_i^-,\quad
                    U+\sum_{i\in J}d_i^+\right].
\end{equation}
The base relation accepts any bounded radius satisfying $E_i\leq\rho_i^2$;
the native builder selects the minimum. A larger valid radius only
decreases its lower endpoint and increases its upper endpoint. Therefore
$\ell_i\leq\bar\ell_i\leq T^6b_i\leq\bar u_i\leq u_i$;
adding the canonical corrections remains conservative even if the original
proof used a nonminimal radius. Enforcing minimality in the refinement
relation is necessary for this argument. The verifier first replays the
complete compact proof backbone, then checks every refinement proof, common
context, approved centers, unique indices and the 16-row cap before applying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:refined-interval}. A correction cannot authorize a decision
without that backbone. Extra public corrections create additional output
leakage; they do not strengthen the privacy claim.

The ordering has a precise, limited justification. Put
$w_i=\bar u_i-\bar\ell_i\geq0$ and $W=U-L$. For any fixed unique set $J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refinement-width}
 U'-L'=W-\sum_{i\in J}w_i.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $d_i^+-d_i^-=\bar\ell_i-\bar u_i$, independently of the unknown
exact $b_i$. Selecting the largest widths therefore minimizes the resulting
interval width among all subsets of the same cardinality, without inspecting
exact corrections. This does not make it optimal for resolving the sign,
minimizing elapsed time or hiding outputs. A cost change arbitrarily close
to zero can remain inconclusive after the fixed budget, even with a narrow
interval and high assignment agreement.

\subsection{Binding private group moments}
The compact derived leaf authenticates $(H_i,E_i,g_i)$ under one root. A
separate salted commitment binds all private $n_g,S_g$ and the model and row
roots. An independent auditor issues and pins an unpredictable nonzero field
challenge $r$ only after these commitments are fixed. Each preprocessing proof
opens the original and derived row, proves the compact derivation and exact
old membership, and publishes group contributions to
$1+\sum_{d=0}^{D-1}x_{id}r^{d+1}$ hidden by fresh linked private masks. The
verifier checks every catalog index exactly once, the complete adjacent mask
chain and common context. A moment-link relation checks that the folded
contributions equal the corresponding count-and-coordinate-sum polynomials
plus the endpoint-mask difference. \hyperref[sec:protocol-spec]{Appendix~A} gives the
mask construction, challenge lifecycle and concrete integer bounds.
The compact query combines this link with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:group-assigned-change}; it does not accept an unauthenticated
native sum.

For fixed false moments, the discrepancy polynomial has degree at most $D$.
The total fingerprint error for one preprocessing session is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:security}. Checked ranges exclude ambiguity from integer reduction modulo
the field. A preannounced or prover-selected challenge does not provide this
argument. The implementation simulates an independent local auditor; a
production authenticated challenge service and a complete protocol-security
and privacy audit remain outside the present evaluation.

The public verifier reconstructs center-dependent parameters from independently
approved original-space centers. It pins keys, roots, challenge, model and task,
requires complete query coverage, and sums signed endpoints with
arbitrary-precision integers. Public outputs include $A$, per-batch switching
intervals and the resulting decision. Moments, memberships, row values and
masks are witnesses rather than direct plaintext outputs. This does not
guarantee that moments are hidden from inference: Section~\ref{sec:leakage}
shows a concrete recovery attack using permitted coordinate probes.

\subsection{Prediction and auxiliary certificate ranks}
The prediction model uses a 63-dimensional centroid-informed basis or PCA with
residual energy. The 79-dimensional certificate appends 16 training-residual
SVD directions to the frozen prediction basis, at the same 14-bit basis
precision. Its preprocessing, keys and proofs are generated independently.
It does not change approximate assignments or manufacture a new assignment
guarantee. The group proof
certifies a cost-change interval; assignment agreement and assignment-certified
fraction are evaluated separately. No JL tail is used by this profile.

These ranks define a frozen resource profile, not a theoretical optimum.
Rank 63 is inherited from the development predictor; adding a 16-direction
certificate block gives rank 79 while preserving its predictions. A wider
basis trades more preprocessing and query arithmetic for a smaller residual.
The small centroid span has rank at most $k-1$ and fits within the same total
rank. Both representations receive the same rank and precision. Because the
ranks and row budget were chosen during development, only new, frozen-profile
fits can test their generalization. The earlier confirmation used two
additional seed/split configurations; the extended image study uses eight
dataset/seed splits. The post-review sensor and neural-feature studies are
identified separately in the evaluation.
Both bases are fitted on training data and deployed centers only; future
centers and audit rows do not fit them. The appendix specifies their SVD,
reprojection and rounding recipe. The certificate does not require the
auxiliary predictor to accept an update sign.

\subsection{Moments-only and exact alternatives}
Since $b_i\leq0$, an authenticated $A<0$ suffices for improvement.
A stronger projection-free upper bound allows each old group to choose a
new target center independently:
\begin{align}\label{eq:moments-upper}
 U_*={}&\sum_g\min_j\bigl[2\langle S_g,c^0_g-c^1_j\rangle\nonumber\\
 &\qquad+n_g(\|c^1_j\|^2-\|c^0_g\|^2)\bigr].
\end{align}
Then $\Delta\leq U_*\leq A$. Our moments-only baseline authenticates original
rows, exact old groups and moments, with no head or residual-energy
preprocessing. Native construction selects a minimizing target per group;
the circuit authenticates the chosen valid upper bound. Proving target
optimality is unnecessary for a sound negative-upper decision. Nonnegative
upper bounds remain inconclusive, except a public identity update, and
trigger paid exact fallback. An aggregate-only sign and a narrow two-sided
interval are different outputs; their resolution rates and costs are reported
explicitly.

Reusable public-center Full authenticates original rows and proves exact
nearest-center costs for arbitrary future public centers. For an inconclusive
certificate, the harness generates and publicly verifies the required Full
proofs, caching a shared old cost only after its proof exists. All failed
certificate work and new fallback generation belong to the service cost.
Known-family affine instead authenticates an exact score for every center in a
bank fixed before preprocessing. It is exact for approved family subsets and
rejects out-of-bank centers; this is a different information contract from
unknown future centers. PCA receives the same group relation, refinement budget and precision,
not a weaker proof statement.

\subsection{Where reuse can reduce work}
At the arithmetic-relation level, the switching query evaluates $k$ affine
scores in certificate rank $s$, then selects center-pair radii for the private
old group. This costs $O(ks+k^2)$ scalar work per row, in addition to
authentication and range checks; exact Full evaluates $O(kD)$ coordinate
terms per row. An exact-row refinement pays both types of work on that row.
Projection derivation and exact old grouping move $O(NDs+NDk)$ arithmetic
into preprocessing, and public center-dependent parameter construction is
still required per update. These operation counts are not proof-time
predictions: sparsity, constraint encoding, keys, batching and verification
also matter. Reuse is most plausible for multiple requests against a fixed
baseline, moderate $k$, and a small refinement budget. Frequent baseline
replacement, a large number of centers or paid full-catalog fallback can
remove the advantage. The image proof controls at $k\in\{3,5\}$ do not establish scaling
to many centers.

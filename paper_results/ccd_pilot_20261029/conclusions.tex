\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We study reusable zero-knowledge certificates for candidate prototype banks
on a committed feature catalog. Authenticated exact old groups yield an
exact assigned-center change; integer-safe switching bounds and verified
row corrections produce a conservative interval for the original quantized
objective. Unresolved requests require proved exact evaluation, with its
cost retained.

Completed local measurements identify useful reuse regimes and concrete
limits. The archived 1024-record Fashion control takes 42.534 minutes against
101.575 for Full. A newer 64-request warm Fashion pilot takes 23.005 minutes
against 43.885 for the ZKML primitive adapter. Default-rank PCA remains close,
and difficult text requests do not demonstrate a compact-path speed benefit.
Cost-aware planning can avoid unsuccessful proof work, but must account for
key loading and its own native computation.

The later three-fit numerical gate adds paper-derived projection, coreset
and one-bit quantization controls. A fresh four-request Fashion warm pair
takes 2.124 minutes for centroid and 4.730 for Full; equally optimized PCA
takes 2.068 minutes. The cold ZKML primitive adapter is faster in both
later proof cohorts, and all warm text routes select Full. These outcomes
support conditional reuse, not superiority of the fitted basis or of the
service over published systems generally.

These preliminary results support a selective audit mechanism under an
explicit reuse and disclosure contract. They do not establish a universally
superior representation or proving system. The planned bounded evaluation
will examine component effects, amortization and scale with matched
comparators and independent fitted configurations.

\subsection{Paired certificates for a prototype-update decision}\label{sec:paired-method}
An auditor comparing updates needs a decision about
$\Delta_I=\Phi_I(\mathcal C^+)-\Phi_I(\mathcal C^-)$, rather than only two
absolute cost intervals. Subtracting independent intervals gives
$[L^+-U^-,U^+-L^-]$, which can be inconclusive even when the observed
approximation errors are small. We instead subtract center distances before
bounding the omitted residual terms. The stored catalog and Stage~1 relation
remain unchanged.

All vectors below are quantized integers. For the same rounded basis $B$ and
anchor as Eq.~\eqref{eq:protected-integer}, put $G=BB^\top-T^2I$. Direct expansion
gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-identity}
\begin{split}
T^2D(x,c)-T^6\|x-c\|^2={}&2T^2\langle R(x),R(c)\rangle\\
&+(H(x)-H(c))^\top G(H(x)-H(c)).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This is an algebraic identity, not a new projection theorem. For candidate
center $c_a^+$ and baseline center $c_b^-$, write
$Q_{ab}(x)=(H(x)-H(c_a^+))^\top G(H(x)-H(c_a^+))
-(H(x)-H(c_b^-))^\top G(H(x)-H(c_b^-))$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-affine}
P_{ab}(x)=T^2\{D(x,c_a^+)-D(x,c_b^-)\}-Q_{ab}(x).
\end{equation}
The quadratic terms in $H(x)$ cancel in both differences; hence $P_{ab}$ is
affine in the authenticated private head. The verifier derives its coefficients
and constant from the approved full integer centers and pinned basis. The
rounded-Gram correction is evaluated exactly, rather than bounded by the
absolute row-sum defect.

Let $\rho_{ab}=\lceil\|R(c_a^+)-R(c_b^-)\|\rceil$ and let the circuit check
a bounded private $u_i$ with $u_i^2\ge E_i$. Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\begin{align}
\ell_{iab}&=P_{ab}(x_i)-2T^2u_i\rho_{ab},\nonumber\\
v_{iab}&=P_{ab}(x_i)+2T^2u_i\rho_{ab}.\label{eq:paired-pair-bounds}
\end{align}
These bound $T^6(\|x_i-c_a^+\|^2-\|x_i-c_b^-\|^2)$. The uncertainty depends
on the difference of center residuals, not their separate magnitudes.

\begin{proposition}[Paired nearest-center change envelope]\label{prop:paired-envelope}
For every approved pair of center sets, define
\begin{align}
\underline\Delta_I&=\sum_{i\in I}\min_a\max_b\ell_{iab},\nonumber\\
\overline\Delta_I&=\sum_{i\in I}\max_b\min_a v_{iab}.\label{eq:paired-envelope}
\end{align}
Then $\underline\Delta_I\le T^6\Delta_I\le\overline\Delta_I$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:paired-identity} and Cauchy--Schwarz establish the pairwise
inequalities. For a separable matrix $F_{ab}=f_a-g_b$, both
$\min_a\max_b F_{ab}$ and $\max_b\min_a F_{ab}$ equal
$\min_a f_a-\min_b g_b$. Apply monotonicity to the lower and upper pairwise
bounds using the respective orders, then sum over records. The two orders
must not be interchanged for the nonseparable bound matrices.
\end{proof}

The circuit authenticates each $(H_i,E_i)$, checks signed affine arithmetic,
radii, pairwise comparisons and aggregate endpoints. Public coefficients and
radii are independently reconstructed by the verifier; bounded field arithmetic
prevents wraparound. The endpoints decode by $T^6S^2$. An upper endpoint below
zero certifies improvement; a lower endpoint above zero certifies worsening.
An interval $[0,0]$ certifies equal cost, and other intervals containing zero
are inconclusive. Identical center sets give $[0,0]$ with honest evaluation:
diagonal pairs are zero and the min/max envelopes enclose the zero change.
No 98\% or 99\% policy is needed for these sign decisions.

The implementation is \path{code/zk/circuit/bound_paired_change.go}.
For a batch of $q$ rows, $r$ retained coordinates and $k_-,k_+$ centers, the
pair construction requires $O(qk_-k_+r)$ arithmetic, in addition to public-input
range checks and authentication. Thus cancellation can tighten the guarantee
while quadratic dependence on center count increases cost. Higher decision
rates and lower cost must both be measured; neither follows from the identity.

\subsection{Public decision and complete-task exact fallback}\label{sec:paired-fallback}
A public-only auditor first verifies all paired batches against independently
approved baseline/candidate centers, original root, subset coverage and pinned
keys. It sums the signed endpoints and reports improvement, worsening, equality
or inconclusive. A conclusive certificate guarantees the sign of the quantized
original-space cost change, even though an internal approximation is used. It
does not certify every individual assignment.

For an inconclusive update, the implemented composed auditor can select and
verify exact Full proofs for the baseline and that candidate. It requires
matching original roots, dimensions, catalog coverage and quantization, and
compares the resulting exact aggregate costs. A baseline result can be reused
across several fallback candidates. Only complete update tasks are refined;
the auditor reads no original rows, private assignments or per-row uncertainty
flags. Exact aggregate baseline/candidate costs become public when fallback is
used. The workflow is implemented in
\path{code/experiments/review_public_decision_audit.py}.

The experiments exercise this selection and fresh replay using saved exact
proofs. They do not measure an online service that generates fallback proofs
on demand. Fallback publisher costs must be charged as additional exact work;
the composed auditor's replay time alone is not that end-to-end cost.
